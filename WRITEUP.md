# DVProject_Joseph_Cartafalsa

  My original idea was a horizon chart concurrently showing telescope viewing conditions for Venus, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn from NYC over ten years, with peak heights driven by closeness/elongation and overlaid by factors like ring tilt and season. To get the data into D3 I wrote small Python parsers (parse_horizons.py, csv_to_js.py) that pull daily ephemerides from JPL HORIZONS, strip the SOE/EOE block to get just CSV with no headers, and inline each CSV as JS so the page works without fetch. Plotting raw APmag for Saturn surfaced an M shape between every opposition, a big spike at opposition plus a smaller one halfway in, which is possibly whats called the Seeliger opposition surge seen at both opposition and superior conjunction. To look into any correlations I built D3 multi-axis comparison charts overlaying APmag, ObsSub-LAT (ring tilt angle), surface brightness, and i at one point included airmass for Saturn, Jupiter, and Venus, which was meant to factor into optimal viewing periods by showing when they were high in the sky with minimal atmosphere for light to travel to, but it confirmed the effect ispossibly ring-driven (Jupiter shows a clean sawtooth, no M) and that Saturn's ten-year brightness follows its 2032 ring-tilt peak.

  The correlation charts were a useful tangent but my proposal was about timing observing windows, so for the final visualization I simplifiedthe levels of abstraction instead. In used one row per planet, ten years across, only the peak APmag windows drawn on the y axis, each row thresholded at its own median magnitude so peak heights within a row are directly comparable. Dim periods are dropped, leaving discrete bright bumps at each opposition or greatest elongation. What I wanted to visualize was the relative differences between observing windows, which the stacked rows show directly: Saturn's slow ring-driven brightening, Jupiter's regular ~13-month opposition cycle, Mars's irregular biennial-sh cycle, and Venus's twice-per-1.6-year apparitions so to speak, side by side
