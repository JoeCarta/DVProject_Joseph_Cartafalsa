Saturn: APmag, ObsSub-LAT, and Surface Brightness over 20 years

2020 to 2040, three stacked panels with a shared time axis. Lower magnitude = brighter.

APmag dips at opposition each year and again at superior conjunction, making an M-shape per orbit. The overall brightness envelope tracks ObsSub-LAT (ring tilt angle): Saturn is brightest around 2032 when the rings face Earth directly and dimmest near 2025 and 2039 when they go edge-on. Surface brightness follows the same pattern.

Jupiter: APmag, ObsSub-LAT, and Surface Brightness over 20 years

Same three panels as Saturn above, for comparison.

Jupiter shows a clean sawtooth with no secondary dip, confirming Saturn's double-dip comes from ring backscatter rather than a general planetary effect. Jupiter's ObsSub-LAT barely moves since its axial tilt is only 3 degrees.
